How it works

Day 1, impact. Each planet is a set of particles with an iron core and a rock mantle. Gravity uses a Barnes–Hut tree; pressure, shocks and heating use smoothed particle hydrodynamics (SPH), the method in the paper, with a simplified equation of state.

After day 1, disk. As in the paper, every particle is sorted by its orbit: part of the planet, bound in the disk, or escaping. The planet becomes one body. Inside the Roche limit (dashed circle) tides keep debris from clumping and it drains onto the planet. Outside it, debris that is gravitationally bound sticks together into moonlets, then one Moon.

Limits. This is a flat 2D toy with 3D gravity, about 100× fewer particles than the paper, and simple stand-ins for disk gas and viscosity. Expect the right sequence of events, not the paper's numbers. Results change from run to run.
